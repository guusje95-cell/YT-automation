# Car niche — research (2026-10-27)

Method: same scanners as the space decision. `niche_scan` (8 sub-niches, past-year search results, channel sizes) and
`topic_scan` (20 car topics, two viewer-style queries each, top-8 demand, small-channel breakouts). Raw data:
`research/niche_metricscars_2026-10-27.csv`, `research/raw/niche_scancars_2026-10-27.json`,
`research/topic_scan_2026-10-27_cars.md`. Public counts at scan time; decision aid, not forecast.

Pattern: a dramatic *story* (downfall, feud, scandal, record) — exactly our story engine — not car reviews.

## Money (Estimate)
Auto advertisers (insurance, finance, parts) lift CPMs; reported automotive CPM ~$10–20 and one anecdotal channel
RPM ~$5 (vidIQ and creator roundups; not verified data). Likely somewhat above our space estimate ($3–8 RPM).

## The hard part: visuals and rights (Fact + judgment)
- Manufacturer press images/video are typically **editorial-use only**; monetized YouTube may count as commercial use
  (Toyota, Sony Honda Mobility, Audi press terms). Not safe to automate on.
- Race/brand footage → Content ID and manual claims; F1 (FOM) notably strict.
- Reused/"inauthentic" content policy (renamed July 2025) punishes channels that only re-cut others' footage.
- Workable, licence-clean sources: Wikimedia Commons car photos (CC BY / BY-SA / public domain, credit required),
  public-domain archival photos (pre-1930 US, US-government crash tests from NHTSA), data visuals from company
  filings (SEC EDGAR — already in the pipeline from the backup-niche work), maps/timelines, and our own renders.
  Wikimedia rate-limited this server during the check, so asset depth per topic is still to be measured.

## Verdict
Viable and arguably more monetizable than space, **if** we stay in story formats (downfalls, feuds, scandals,
records) built from licence-clean photos + data visuals + our own graphics. Not viable: footage-driven formats
(F1, rebuilds, test drives). Best first pilots: *Fastest car from every brand / speed records* (data-driven,
2.0M breakout), *Worst cars ever made* (9k-sub channel at 686k), or a corporate-drama story (Jaguar/Europe vs China EVs).
